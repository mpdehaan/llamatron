Scenes ​

A scene is a row of Arrange: the clips that play together, with settings for the whole section. This page covers the Scene page.

Scene Length ​

A scene starts all of its clips together and has no length of its own: a scene lasts as long as its longest clip that doesn't loop, times the scene's repeats.

Scene Settings ​

On Arrange, ENTER on a scene's name opens the Scene page.

Under the Name, a box of tabs holds the rest, as on the clip page (see Clips for the keys): Settings, with Playing (Section, Repeat, Launch together, Scale, Groove, Tempo); Velocity, the Velocity field across its top and the two ramps as columns under it; Tempo Ramps; Shapes; Reset Draws, the draws the scene starts afresh (see Draws); More Generators, the Generators field. A scene has no built-in generators.

SettingWhat it does
NameThe scene's name.
SectionThe kind of section the scene is, such as verse, chorus or solo, chosen from a list, or (none). It changes nothing about how the scene plays.
ScaleA scale for the scene's clips in place of the song's. Bksp goes back to the song's.
GrooveA groove for the scene's clips in place of the song's, or off to play them straight whatever the song or a track chooses. A track's own groove still wins over the scene's, but not over the scene's off. Bksp goes back to the song's.
TempoThe percentage of the song's tempo the scene plays at, from 10% to 400%, shown with the tempo it makes, such as 125% (150 BPM). 100% is the song's tempo; no tempo is slower than 20 BPM or faster than 999, Live's limits. Bksp goes back to the song's. Changing the song's tempo moves every scene with it.
RepeatPlays the whole scene this many times, from 1 to 999.
GeneratorsThe generators every clip of the scene runs, after each clip's own, edited as a clip's are. See Generators.
Launch togetherFor real-time playback only, and on by default: when a clip above sends a start to any clip of this scene, every clip of the scene starts together. When off, each clip starts only when the clip directly above it sends a start, and Arrange marks the scene with ┆ after its name. Space or ENTER toggles it. See Playing Live.
Ramp In, Ramp OutOn the Tempo Ramps tab, two columns that move the tempo gradually at the scene's start and at its end. See Tempo Ramps.
VelocityOn the Velocity tab, across its top: how loud every clip of the scene plays, as a percentage, from 0% to 200%. Blank is 100%. See Velocity and Velocity Ramps.
Ramp In, Ramp OutOn the Velocity tab, two columns under the Velocity that fade the scene in from its start and out to its end. See Velocity and Velocity Ramps.
Density, ArticulateOn the Shapes tab, columns each with a Start, End, Over and Then that shape every clip of the scene across the whole scene, unless a clip sets its own shape or a generator in its chains does. See Shapes.

The page shows how long the scene lasts, and any problem, such as every clip looping. Turning on a loop that leaves a scene with no clip that ends opens a Scene loop errors dialog naming the scenes to fix, and exporting while any scene is in that state does the same.

Tempo Ramps ​

A band that eases into a song, a DJ set that creeps a few beats a minute faster over ten minutes, a last chorus that slows to a stop: each is a tempo that moves gradually instead of jumping. A scene has two ramps for that. On the Tempo Ramps tab, Ramp In starts at the scene's first bar, and Ramp Out is counted back from the scene's last bar. Without a ramp, a scene plays at one tempo from start to end.

Each column, Ramp In and Ramp Out, holds Start, End and Over:

SettingWhat it does
StartThe percentage of the scene's tempo the ramp starts from, 10% to 400%, shown with the tempo it makes. The scene's tempo is the Tempo field's, or else the song's. Blank is 100%, the scene's tempo.
EndThe percentage of the scene's tempo the ramp reaches. Blank is 100%.
OverHow many bars the ramp takes, from 1 to 9999. Blank, there is no ramp.

A ramp needs its bars and a start or an end, and Bksp on a field makes the field blank again. The tempo changes at every step, the set's step size, a sixteenth note unless changed, so a ramp is a slope and never a stair of bars: a note in the middle of a bar is already a little faster than the note before. A ramp of 1 BPM over 64 bars is as smooth as a ramp of 40 BPM over 2. A scene that repeats counts its bars across all of its repeats together, so one ramp runs once through the whole scene. Where the bars of the two ramps overlap, Ramp In wins. Between the ramps the scene plays at its own tempo, and neither ramp changes anything after the scene: the next scene starts at its own tempo, so a tempo that should stay where a ramp took it is set as the next scene's Tempo.

In an example scenario, a song plays at 120 and a scene of 8 bars sets Ramp In's Start to 50% and Over to 2, and Ramp Out's End to 75% and Over to 1. The scene starts at 60, half its tempo, reaches 120 as bar 3 starts, plays bars 3 to 7 at 120, and slows through bar 8 to end at 90. A first scene that builds slowly might ramp from 50% to 100% over all its bars.

examples/by_genre/electronic/minimal_techno.yml climbs from 126 to 132 across its last five scenes, each scene's Tempo Ramp In taking all of the scene's bars to reach the tempo the next scene starts at, and its last scene's Tempo Ramp Out slows the final 2 bars to a stop.

The song's length, each scene's length and each clip's length on Arrange are measured through the ramps, as are the MIDI files, the WAV file and real-time playback. A Live set follows a ramp in Arrangement View only: see Exporting.

Velocity and Velocity Ramps ​

A band plays a verse quietly and a chorus loudly, a song fades in from nothing, and a last chorus fades away. A scene's Velocity is the first of these: every note of the scene plays at that percentage of the velocity the note was written with, so accents and soft notes keep their proportions. 100% is the notes as written, 50% is half as hard, 0% is as soft as a note can be, and a value above 100% plays harder, each note stopping at the top MIDI velocity.

Velocities stack: an instrument's, a track's, a scene's and a clip's each scale what the one before gives, as faders do on a mixer. In an example scenario, a scene at 80% holds a clip at 50%, so the clip plays at 40% of what is written; 0% anywhere is silence.

The two ramps are the fades. On the Velocity tab, Ramp In starts at the scene's first bar and Ramp Out is counted back from the scene's last bar. Each is a percentage of the level the clip plays at, so a ramp from 0% to 100% fades in from silence to that level, whatever it is.

Each column, Ramp In and Ramp Out, holds Start, End and Over:

SettingWhat it does
StartThe percentage of the level the ramp starts from, 0% to 200%. Blank is 100%, the level itself.
EndThe percentage of the level the ramp reaches. Blank is 100%.
OverHow many bars the ramp takes, from 1 to 9999. Blank, there is no ramp.

A ramp needs its bars and a start or an end. Between the two ramps the scene plays at its Velocity. Where the two overlap, the ramp in wins. A scene that repeats counts a ramp's bars across all of its repeats together, as a tempo ramp does.

In an example scenario, a scene of 8 bars has Velocity at 80%, Ramp In's Start at 0% and Over at 2, and Ramp Out's End at 50% and Over at 1. The scene fades in from silence to 80% across its first 2 bars, plays bars 3 to 7 at 80%, and falls to 40%, half of its 80%, through bar 8.

A clip has the same seven fields on its clip page. The clip's Velocity stacks on the scene's, blank being 100%. Each ramp field a clip sets wins over the scene's, since two fades together would fade twice, and a ramp field the clip leaves blank takes the scene's, shown as 50% (from scene). When a clip gives a ramp its own bars, the bars are counted over that clip's own play: from where the clip starts, and back from where the clip ends, which for a looping clip is the end of the scene.

A Shapes generator's Swell, in a clip's chains, scales the same notes again, on top of the scene's and the clip's velocity, so that one Shapes generator can add accents or a shape of its own to many clips.
