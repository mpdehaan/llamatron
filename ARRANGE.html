Arrange ​

Arrange is where a song is put together: scenes as rows, tracks as columns, and a clip where a row and a column meet. This page covers the Arrange page itself. Tracks, Scenes and Clips cover the settings of each, and Playing Live covers playing the song.

Grid Layout ​

Press A on the main screen to open the current song's arrangement. The track names run across the top, and the first column holds the scene names, each with its repeat count and length. Scenes play from top to bottom. A new song starts with a main track, main1, a drum track, drum1, and scene-1; new main tracks are numbered main2, main3 and on, new drum tracks drum2, drum3 and on, and new scenes as scene-2, until they are renamed on their own pages.

The main tracks come first, then a green double line, then the drum tracks, each group in the order the set keeps them. I (insert) adds a scene after the scene under the cursor, or a track of the cursor's track's kind after it, so the grid draws no + row or column of its own and leaves the room to the clips. A song keeps at least one main track and one drum track, so each kind always has a track to insert after; a set saved before this, with no tracks of one kind, shows a + in that group's heading, where I makes its first track. Each new track comes with an instrument of its own, named after it, which plays through what the File/Global page's Track Inst. Default chooses: Sampled (FluidSynth)'s grand piano or its drums, LlamaSynth's Basic or MechaDrum, or the first MIDI device found; a copied track gets a copy of its instrument, so that the two can be changed apart. A track is a melodic track or a drum track from when it is made, and never changes, so a clip on a drum track only ever plays a drum pattern: choosing a pattern for an empty cell offers only patterns of its track's kind, and a new pattern made there is already the right kind.

Light blue lines run between the tracks and under each scene, so that the grid is easy to follow, and green lines frame the headings: the double line right of the scene names, the line under the track names, and the double line between the main and the drum tracks. A double-lined box inside a cell marks whatever the cursor is on: a clip, a scene, a track name, or an empty group's +. Under the track names a + column holds nothing, so the arrows pass over it. The arrows move the box around the grid. When there are more tracks or scenes than fit, the clips scroll while the track names and the scene column stay in place. An empty cell is blank until the cursor reaches it, when it shows + and add.

Under each scene is a bar of its length against the longest scene's, and under each clip a bar of the clip's length against its scene's. A clip that fills its scene, or loops, has a full bar, and a clip that ends early has a shorter one.

Fracture ​

A song often plays one pattern in several clips: every verse shares one bass line, so that a change to the bass line changes every verse. That is what a song being written wants. A finished song may want the opposite, such as a second verse whose bass line differs by a few notes from the first, or the sections of a song the Song Wizard made edited one by one.

F asks "Do you really want to give all clips unique patterns?", and on YES every clip of the song gets a pattern of its own. Reading the scenes from the top and each scene's tracks from the left, the first clip to play a pattern keeps the pattern, and each later clip that plays the same pattern plays a copy, numbered as any duplicate is: a pattern called bass used by three clips leaves bass, bass-2 and bass-3. The copies stay in the pattern's group, the song sounds exactly as before, and one Ctrl+Z undoes the whole thing. The purpose is to end the sharing between clips, so that a variation of one clip is easy to make. A clip that plays several patterns gets its own copy of each pattern another clip plays, and a pattern named twice in the clip's own list stays one pattern there.

Details View ​

D shows the details view, which reveals how a song is put together, and D again goes back to the usual grid. Under each scene's bar, each clip's bar and each track's name are the chains it runs, in the order they run, each written chain: name, then a + for adding another, then each setting that isn't its default, on a line of its own, such as scale: for a scale other than the song's, morph: other, direction: pendulum, density: 25→100 or inst: piano. A cell with nothing of its own says stock. Columns widen to fit. Every key works as it does in the usual grid.

The details are also where a cell's chains are edited. With the cursor on a track's name, a scene's name or a clip, Tab locks into that cell's details. This works in the usual grid too, where it opens that one cell's details. The cell's box turns green and the first line that names something is picked, shown in brackets. ↑↓ choose a line. ENTER on a chain, a scale, an instrument or a groove opens its page, and ESC comes back to the locked cell. ENTER on + offers the set's chains and adds the one chosen to this cell alone. Bksp on a chain: line takes that chain out of this cell alone; the chain itself stays in the set and in every other cell that runs it. Tab again or ESC leaves the cell, and D leaves it and closes every detail.

Scene View ​

V on a scene's name opens Scene View, a window over Arrange that lays out every note the scene plays, track by track, on one piano roll: each track in a colour of its own, the drum tracks in the lanes at the foot, the chord on each beat named above, and at the right each track's chains and settings, as the details view shows them. It opens on the patterns as written, before any generator, groove or draw changes them, with a purple border.

V switches to the notes as played, after every chain, groove, strum, shape and draw, with a blue border, so that the two can be compared, showing one take of the song's random parts. The arrows move the cursor, and the line under the roll names the notes under it. PgUp and PgDn jump a bar, - and = make each step narrower or wider, and [ and ] scroll the tracks at the right. ESC closes it.

Each chord heard is named in the scene's scale with its Roman numeral. A chord that leads to another chord of the key is numbered by the chord it leads to: in C major, D7 is V7/V, E7 is V7/vi and F#dim7 is vii°7/V. A chord borrowed from the minor key is numbered by its root and spelled with a flat, such as iv for Fm and ♭VII for Bb.
