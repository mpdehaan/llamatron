# Music Box and Lullabies

Nursery tunes and cradle songs, as a mechanical music box or a quiet nursery
arrangement plays them. Principles only.

## The Lullaby

- **Meter**: triple or compound (3/4, 6/8) for the cradle songs, which rock;
  plain 2/4 or 4/4 for the nursery tunes and the rounds, which walk.
  (practice)
- **Tempo**: slow, about 60 to 90, never hurried; a gentle slowing at the end.
  (practice)
- **Tune**: a narrow range, usually within a sixth to an octave; simple
  contours that repeat; phrases that fall more than they rise; mostly steps
  and the notes of the home chord; long pauses between phrases. Listeners
  across cultures pick a lullaby out by its slowness, narrow range and
  falling, repeating shapes. (measured)
- **Phrases**: short and even, 2 or 4 bars, in pairs: a question and an
  answer. Whole tunes are 8 to 16 bars, in the shapes A A B A or A B A, with a
  middle that only moves the same idea a step down or up. Many tunes use five
  or six notes. (practice)
- **Repetition is the point**: a bar is often said twice at once, and a phrase
  often returns unchanged. The tune is its own hook: there is no verse to hold
  it back. (practice)
- **Rounds**: some nursery tunes are built so that a second voice may start
  the tune 2 bars after the first and a third after that, every phrase fitting
  over one or two chords. A music box or two instruments can play one as a
  canon. (practice)
- **Chords**: major keys; I and V nearly throughout, IV as the one color; a
  drone on the home note and fifth suits many. (practice)

## The Music Box as an Instrument

- **Range**: a small movement has 18 teeth and about two octaves; a larger one
  30 teeth and three octaves. Some teeth share a pitch, so a small comb may
  have only about 14 different notes, and those of one key: no accidentals
  outside it. (measured)
- **At once**: thin. Two notes together on a small comb; a chord is a bass
  note and a tune note, or the chord broken. (measured)
- **No sustain**: a plucked tooth rings and dies and cannot be held or damped,
  so long notes are written as a note and silence, or filled with a broken
  chord; quarters and eighths, not whole notes. (measured)
- **Repeated notes**: one tooth cannot be plucked again quickly (about four
  times a second at most, low notes less), so fast repeats are avoided, shared
  between teeth of one pitch, or the tempo is lowered. (measured)
- **Low notes are few and weak**; the tune sits high, the bass is a sparse
  note on the strong beats. (measured)
- **Length and looping**: a small cylinder turns in about 15 seconds, so the
  tune is one short strain that must sound whole when it comes round again.
  (measured)
- **Arranging**: tune on top, clear; under it a single bass note on beat one
  and a broken chord rising after it (in three: bass, chord note, chord note);
  a small turn or a rolled pair of notes as the only ornament; everything in
  one key. (practice)
- **Winding down**: the spring runs out, so the last turn slows evenly and
  stops mid-phrase or on the final note. Nothing is louder or softer by
  intention: dynamics are flat, with small unevenness between teeth.
  (practice)
- **A nursery arrangement** beside the box: celesta, glockenspiel, harp or
  soft piano on the same writing, with a held string or pad note allowed
  underneath. (practice)
