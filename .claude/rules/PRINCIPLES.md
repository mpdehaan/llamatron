# What Decides a Close Call

How the user weighs one way of building against another, where the rules don't settle it. `docs/WHY_LLAMATRON.md` says what
llamatron is for.

- **Someone who never reads the manual still gets around.** Buttons and on-screen choices over remembered keys; every key shown
  where it works; a field shows its kind (`▾`) and its real value (`A Minor (from scene)`). The least surprising behaviour wins,
  judged musically and against familiar apps.
- **Consistency over novelty.** One key, one meaning; one way of moving, choosing and confirming; a new thing looks like its
  nearest existing thing. Where a feature already does what's asked, say so first.
- **What people see is current.** Every visible change carries its docs; screenshots and the website are updated before a push,
  and the website never leads the code.
- **Demos are music first**: starting points people enjoy and learn from. A demo with one song keeps its patterns at the set
  level, so another song can reuse them. One is rewritten only with the user's say-so; new demo ideas wait at the bottom of the
  queue.
- **Bugs are understood**: reproduced, fixed at the cause, explained and held by a test.
- **Progress with every reply**: agreed work built before the next questions, which offer lettered choices and a recommendation.
- **Defaults steer toward good music, and nothing is walled off.** A composing screen arrives filled with choices that sound good
  untouched and still lets a person choose anything; an option never makes the plain case harder.
- **What is rearranged stays musically right.** A setting lives where moving, copying or reconfiguring the notes carries it along:
  a mark on a step travels with the step.
- **Headroom is the budget, and timing is never good enough.** Speed is not judged by what can be heard; every millisecond of the
  render, the real-time chain or a key press comes out of the margin that keeps time steady under load. Keep the program as tight
  as it can be: a slower change needs a reason the user accepts, a feature's time cost is measured before and after, and work done
  only when needed, as late as possible, wins.
