# Hooks

- A hook is whatever a listener carries away: usually a short phrase of melody with its words, but also a riff, a rhythm, a drum
  figure, a sound, or a stop. A song often has several: the chorus's sung hook, an instrumental hook heard in the intro and between
  sections, and a rhythmic hook in the groove. (practice)
- Tunes that stick tend to share three things: a quicker pace, an overall shape that is common and easy to follow (rising then
  falling, or the reverse), and inside that plain shape one or two unusual moves, a leap larger than expected or a repeated note
  where a step was expected. Ordinary enough to hold, odd enough to notice. (measured, as a tendency and not a recipe)
- Short is strong: a hook is commonly one or two bars, a handful of notes, with a rhythm that could be clapped and recognized. The
  rhythm identifies a hook at least as much as its pitches do. (practice)
- Repetition makes a hook, and exact repetition wears it out. The usual pattern is to state it, state it again, vary it (higher,
  or with a changed ending), and return: the shapes AABA and AAAB over four phrases. (practice)
- Placement: the sung hook at the start or the end of the chorus, on strong beats and long notes; the instrumental hook in the
  intro, after each chorus, and under the ending. Hearing the hook before the first verse makes the chorus feel familiar the first
  time it arrives. (practice)
- A hook needs room: the bars around it are plainer, other players thin out or double it, and the drums often mark its arrival.
  A second line fighting the hook weakens both. (practice)
- A hook should be singable: mostly steps and small leaps, within about an octave, sitting where an untrained voice is comfortable.
  (practice)
