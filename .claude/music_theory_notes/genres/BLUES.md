# Blues

The twelve-bar chorus throughout, three dominant-seventh chords, the tune's lowered third and seventh against them. The singer's
line takes about two bars and an instrument answers in the next two: call and answer is the form itself. Starts on the turnaround,
the last four bars of the twelve, and ends on a turnaround walked down to a held chord. (practice)

- **Shuffle** and **driving shuffle**: the long-short pair on every beat, the bass walking; the second harder on the backbeat.
- **Texas shuffle**: a looser, swinging shuffle with the guitar forward.
- **Slow blues**: twelve-eight time, room for long answers, the drums on the ride with soft ghost notes.
- **Jump blues**: fast, swung, horns riffing behind the singer.
- **Minor blues**: the same twelve bars on minor chords, darker and slower.

## Blues Rock

- A **power trio**, guitar, bass and drums, each free: the bass plays lines, not roots, and the drums answer the guitar. Louder
  and longer than the blues, the twelve bars kept. (practice)
- The lead's phrasing is the style: short clipped phrases with space after each, bends that rise to the note and stop short,
  a phrase of three notes repeated and turned, major and minor pentatonic mixed in one line, rhythm invented afresh each
  chorus. A solo builds over several choruses from low and sparse to high and dense. (practice)
- Heavier descendants add a riff under the verse in place of the shuffle, slower tempos and more weight, and open the middle
  for improvising. (practice)
