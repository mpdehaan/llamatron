# Electronic

Built by adding and taking away layers every eight or sixteen bars; sections are builds, drops and breakdowns more than verses
and choruses. Drums first for sixteen bars or more in dance styles, and stripped down the same way at the end. (practice)

- **House**: kick on every beat, off-beat hi-hat, a clap on two and four, chords stabbing off the beat.
- **Techno** and **minimal techno**: the same pulse, harder and barer, change by small degrees.
- **Trance**: long builds, a breakdown with the tune alone, then everything back.
- **Hi-nrg** and **italo**: fast, octave bass, bright synth hooks, song forms with choruses.
- **Electro**: a broken kick pattern from a drum machine, no four on the floor, robotic and dry.
- **Synth-pop**: pop songs on synthesizers: verses, choruses, a lead hook first.
- **Drum and bass**: a fast chopped breakbeat over a bass at half the speed.
- **Big beat**: heavy breakbeats at a middle tempo, riffs and drops.
- **Dubstep**: half-time drums, the snare on three, a bass that moves in the gaps; chords first, drums last.
- **Downtempo** and **ambient**: slow or no pulse, chords and textures first, fading out.
- **Chiptune**: few voices, fast arpeggios standing in for chords, a lead tune throughout.

## IDM

Three approaches recur among the style's founders, and a subgenre could offer each as a way of playing. (practice, from what the
makers have said of their methods and what analysts have written)

- **The system**: rhythm made by rules the musician builds, which put out patterns inside set limits and are steered while they
  run. Any detail can change at the last moment; pieces move slowly by small changes to one rhythm track, pass through several
  time signatures, and stretch a beat until its origin is unrecognizable. The lesson: bounded chance, slow morphing, and several
  meters at once, slipping against each other a step at a time.
- **The worked loop**: a plain loop or break, cut and changed almost past recognition, at jungle tempos near 170. Written in a
  tracker, row by row, which makes very fast rolls (up to sixty-fourth notes, speeding and slowing) and one-off edits natural.
  Against the frantic drums stand slow, simple, singable tunes and unresolved, drifting chords, and often prepared or detuned
  keyboard sounds. The lesson: the contrast is the style, calm melody over restless rhythm.
- **The player**: break chopping so tight that it sounds like one impossible drummer, joined to a virtuoso bass played live, the
  bass carrying chords and tune at once, with jazz harmony. Programming that sounds chaotic and is strictly ordered, sometimes in
  several time signatures at once. The lesson: programmed drums imitate a drummer's phrasing even at inhuman speed, and a real
  melodic instrument on top keeps the music a performance.
- Shared by all three: the drums are the lead instrument; rhythm changes inside a bar at a scale smaller than the sixteenth; no
  two bars need match; and something steady, a pad, a tune or a bass, is always there for the listener to hold.
