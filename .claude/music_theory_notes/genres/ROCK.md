# Rock

Guitars, bass and drums with a backbeat; the riff and the chorus are the song. Verse and chorus forms of eight-bar sections, a
bridge or a solo late. Major and minor keys with the flat seventh chord borrowed freely. (practice)

- **Rock and roll**: twelve-bar or three-chord songs, fast, a shuffle or a straight eighth feel, a drum fill in, a hard stop.
- **British invasion**: tight songs, sung harmonies, a counted or filled start, clear endings; chords richer than three.
- **Folk rock**: strummed or picked guitar first, light drums, the lead instrument sparing; held endings.
- **Hard rock**: the riff first, on guitar, then drums and bass together; heavy backbeat, big fills, a big finish.
- **Arena rock**: built in layers toward a large chorus; the hook stated before the verse; solos; a big finish.
- **Punk**: fast, counted in or straight in, everyone at once, few fills, no ghost notes, a hard stop, hardly a solo.
- **New wave**: drums and bass or keys first, tight and dry, eighth-note bass, synth hooks; held or riff endings.
- **Rock ballad**: keys or a clean guitar alone first, the band entering at a chorus, slowing at the end.

## Ways of Playing Rock, Beyond the Subgenres

Each is a set of habits that could become a subgenre or settings of one. (practice, from lessons and analyses of the styles)

- **Clean and conversational**: a clean guitar played with the fingers, answering every sung line with a short fill of chord
  tones, double stops and slides; dynamics from touch, quiet verses, the rhythm section light and steady, a long melodic solo at
  the end that builds from the fills already heard. The voice half-spoken. Folk, country and jazz chords inside rock.
- **Slow and spacious**: tempos far below other rock, near 60 to 70; a chord held for many bars, sometimes most of a minute; long
  instrumental introductions that add one part at a time; sections that would fill a short song stretched by repetition into a
  long one; a slow, singing guitar solo of few notes with long bends; keyboards holding pads; whole songs joined into suites.
- **Heavy blues, light and shade**: a riff of one or two bars in guitar and bass together, with holes of silence that the drums
  fill; drums huge, behind the beat, with triplet figures on the kick; riffs that turn against the bar while the drums keep four;
  loud electric sections set against quiet acoustic ones in the same song, in steps rather than slopes.
- **Swinging hard rock**: the riff is a shuffle or a boogie at high speed, swung about 60 percent, drums from big-band swing;
  rhythm guitar full of small fills between chords; bright, major, playful; a short flashy solo; backing voices high above.
- **Two guitars, one loose and one tight**: a rhythm guitar tight, simple and punk-like low down, a lead guitar bluesy and singing
  above, the two rarely playing the same thing; a clean arpeggio figure as an intro hook; long outro solos.
- **Southern and twin lead**: two lead guitars playing the tune together a third or a sixth apart, moving through the scale so
  the interval is sometimes major and sometimes minor; major and minor pentatonic mixed; two drummers playing different,
  interlocking parts so the rhythm section is a lead voice; long instrumentals; an organ.
