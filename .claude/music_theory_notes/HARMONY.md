# Harmony

- Most popular music uses three to five chords from one key. In a major key the chords on the first, fourth and fifth steps are
  major and carry most songs; the chord on the sixth step, minor, is the usual fourth chord; those on the second and third add
  gentler colors. (practice)
- Chords have jobs: **home** (the first step), **away** (the fourth, the second) and **wanting home** (the fifth). A progression is
  a trip among them, and where a section's last chord leaves the listener, at home or wanting it, decides how the next section
  arrives. (practice)
- A loop of four chords, one a bar, is the commonest engine; the same four in another order have another character because the
  starting chord and the last chord change. Starting a loop on the minor sixth step makes a major key sound sad without changing
  key. (practice)
- **Borrowed chords** come from the minor key of the same name: the flat seventh, flat sixth and flat third major chords, and the
  minor fourth. The flat seventh is the mark of much rock; the minor fourth of wistful pop. (practice)
- **Sections and chords**: a verse often circles home; a pre-chorus moves away and ends wanting home; a chorus arrives home on its
  first bar or withholds home until its title lands; a bridge commonly starts on the fourth or the sixth step and ends on the fifth.
  A chorus on the same chords as its verse leans on melody and instruments for contrast. (practice)
- **Rate of change**: chords usually change once a bar or once every two; quickening the changes near a section's end raises
  tension, and one held chord under a groove (funk, dance) throws all the weight onto rhythm. (practice)
- **The blues** treats all three main chords as dominant sevenths and keeps its twelve-bar order; the tune's lowered third against
  the chord's raised third is the sound itself, not a mistake. (practice)
- **Voicing**: which notes of a chord, in what order and how spread. Moving each note the smallest distance to the next chord makes
  parts smooth; guitar shapes, keyboard hands and horn sections each have habitual spreads. The bass usually takes the root on the
  first beat and walks or leaps to the next root. An inversion, another chord note in the bass, smooths a bass line. (practice)
- Rules are habits of a style. Using a progression against its genre's habit is a way to sound fresh, and the notes of each genre
  say what its habits are so that bending one is a choice. (judgment)
