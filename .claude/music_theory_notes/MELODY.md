# Melody

## What a Melody Is Made Of

- **Contour**: the shape over a phrase. The common shapes are an arch, a descent, a rise to a late peak, and a level line with one
  excursion. A phrase usually has one high point, and a section usually has one highest note, saved for where it matters. (practice)
- **Motion**: mostly steps, with a few leaps. A leap is commonly followed by a step back the other way, filling the gap it opened.
  Large leaps upward carry effort and lift; downward ones release. (practice)
- **Rhythm**: a small stock of rhythmic cells, repeated. A melody is recognized by its rhythm with the pitches changed more readily
  than by its pitches with the rhythm changed. Notes that start off the beat push; long notes on strong beats settle. (practice)
- **Range**: a sung tune stays within about an octave to a tenth; an instrument's tune ranges wider and may run longer without
  breath. (practice)
- **Chord tones and the notes between**: notes on strong beats mostly belong to the chord of the moment, and the notes between pass
  or lean. A note that leans on a strong beat and then resolves by step is where much of a tune's feeling comes from. (practice)

## Phrases

- Melodies come in phrases of two or four bars with breath between, often paired as a question that ends away from home and an
  answer that ends on it. (practice)
- Four phrases commonly follow AABA, AABB, ABAB or AAAB by their material: enough return to be learned, one change to stay
  interesting. (practice)
- Where a phrase ends matters as much as its notes: ending on the home note closes, ending on another chord tone leaves the door
  open, ending early leaves room for an answer from another player. (practice)

## Ways to Make One

- **From a motif**: a cell of three to five notes, then repeated, moved to another step of the scale (sequence), turned upside
  down, stretched, shortened, or given a new ending. Most durable tunes are a little material worked hard. (practice)
- **From the chords**: outline each chord's tones and join them by steps; good for bass-led and riff-led music. (practice)
- **From a rhythm**: fix a spoken or clapped rhythm first, as the words would give, then find pitches for it. (practice)
- **From a scale walk**: step along the scale from a chosen note, turning at chosen points, with rules for when to leap. (practice)
- **From a row or a rule**: an ordered set of pitches or a process, for music that avoids a key on purpose. (practice)
- **By answering**: write the second phrase as a reply to the first, same rhythm with a different ending, or the same ending
  reached another way. (practice)

## Ways to Keep One Interesting

- Change one thing at a time: keep the rhythm and move the pitches, or keep the pitches and shift the rhythm.
- Start phrases at different points in the bar: on the beat, before it as a pickup, or after it.
- Save the highest note, the longest note and the biggest leap, and do not spend them twice in a section.
- Let the tune rest; a gap is where another player answers and where the listener catches up.
- Verse low, narrow and busy; chorus higher, wider and longer-noted. A bridge takes a new rhythm or starts on a new chord.
- Ornaments, used sparingly: a grace note, a slide into a note, a turn, a held note that swells. (all practice)
