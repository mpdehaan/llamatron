# Drum Machines and Electronic Percussion

Machines are named here, as products that shaped styles; no artist or record is. What a machine could and could not do is why
whole genres sound as they do, so a machine's limits are a style's rules.

## What the Step Sequencer Did to Rhythm

- A bar is sixteen steps, each on or off for each sound, with an **accent** that makes chosen steps louder. Patterns of one bar,
  chained into songs, with an A and a B variation and a fill for each, are how machine music is thought: loop, variation, fill.
  (practice)
- Everything lands exactly on the grid unless **swing** (shuffle) is on: every second sixteenth played late. Swing is given as the
  share of a pair of sixteenths the first one takes: 50 percent is straight, about 66 is a full triplet shuffle, and the values
  between, 54 to 62, are the "feel" settings, a lean too small to hear as a shuffle and large enough to move a body. (practice)
- A machine has two or three loudnesses where a drummer has dozens, so machine grooves get their life from pattern, accent, swing,
  sound choice and tuning, and from muting and unmuting parts. (practice)

## The Machines and What Each Gave

- **Preset rhythm boxes of the 1970s** (the CR-78 and its kin): soft analog ticks and thumps, preset rock, waltz, bossa and shuffle
  patterns with a few variations; the sound of early new wave and synth-pop, used as a texture as much as a drummer.
- **TR-808**: all analog. A long booming kick that can be tuned and held like a bass note, a snappy thin snare, a clap, a cowbell,
  ticking hats. Sixteen-step programming with accent. The base of electro, early hip hop, Miami bass and, later, trap, where the
  kick is the bass line and the hats roll in fast subdivisions.
- **TR-909**: analog drums with sampled cymbals, a hard punching kick, a per-pattern shuffle, and MIDI. The kick on every beat with
  an open hat between is house; the same machine driven harder is techno.
- **LinnDrum** and its forerunner: samples of real drums, tunable, with swing and with quantizing of what is played in on pads. The
  drum sound of 1980s pop and rock: big, clean, exact, a huge snare (often with a cut-off reverb) on two and four, hand claps and
  tambourine layered on, sixteenth-note hats played evenly, tom fills that run down the kit.
- **DMX**: sampled drums with a punchy, dry, hard sound; early hip hop, dance pop and new wave, heavy kick-and-snare patterns.
- **SP-1200** and **MPC60** onward: samplers with pads. Drums taken from records, low-resolution and gritty, played in by hand and
  swung; chopped breaks. The MPC's pads and its swing made the laid-back, late-snare feel of boom bap; its sixteen pads made
  playing a pattern like an instrument normal.
- **HR-16**, **SR-16**, **SR-18**: affordable boxes of realistic sampled kits with preset patterns played in by drummers, each with
  an A and a B part and a fill for each, a loudness-sensitive sound that changes tone with force, and a footswitch to move through
  a song. The songwriter's and the demo's drummer from the late 1980s on: rock, pop and country backing without a kit.
- **Machinedrum**: sixteen tracks, each a small synthesizer chosen from families: models of the classic analog machines, FM
  percussion, sampled acoustic drums, and physical models of struck things. Its sequencer lets any step carry its own value for any
  setting (a parameter lock), so one track can be a different sound on every step; each track has an LFO; kits are kept apart from
  patterns; tracks are muted and brought back as a performance. The instrument of much IDM and minimal techno. Later machines of
  the family added steps that play only on some passes or by chance.

## Electronic Percussion by Style

- **Dance music (house, techno, trance)**: a kick on every beat; a clap or snare on two and four; an open hat on every off-beat; a
  closed hat or shaker in sixteenths, accented for bounce; a ride added at the high point. Parts enter and leave every eight or
  sixteen bars. A build-up is a snare roll that doubles its speed, from quarters to eighths to sixteenths to thirty-seconds, with a
  rising sound and the kick taken out, then a beat of silence, then everything at once. House is straight or lightly swung;
  garage-like styles swing the hats hard. Every layer should have a job; a layer nobody would miss goes. (practice)
- **Electro**: no kick on every beat. A syncopated kick, snare or clap on two and four, cowbell and tom figures, stiff and exact.
- **Breakbeat styles (drum and bass, big beat)**: a drummer's recorded bar or two cut into slices and rearranged; the snare on two
  and four survives the cutting, and the kick moves. Ghost notes come with the break for free.
- **IDM and glitch**: the grid is the material. Steps repeat very fast (ratchets, rolls that speed up or slow down), single hits are
  cut, reversed, pitched and stuttered, patterns of different lengths run against each other and drift, steps play by chance or
  only on some passes, and a bar may change length. A steady element, often the tune or a pad, holds the listener while the drums
  misbehave. Push and pull against the grid and odd groupings (threes and fives across four) replace the backbeat. (practice)
- **1980s pop and rock with a machine**: the pattern is a drummer's, simplified and exact: a strong backbeat, even hats, a fill at
  each eight bars, claps doubling the snare in choruses. The exactness is the sound; humanizing it loses the period. (judgment)

## What This Suggests for llamatron

- A drummer in the wizard could be a **machine** as well as a person: exact timing, few loudnesses, accent and swing in place of
  ghost notes and push or drag, A and B variations with a fill for each. (judgment)
- Swing wants the feel values between straight and triplet, named in percent as the machines name them. (judgment)
- Rolls that change speed, steps by chance, steps on some passes only, and patterns of unequal length are the IDM toolkit, and
  llamatron's generators already hold several. (judgment)
- A kit is a choice apart from a pattern: the same pattern on another machine's sounds is another genre. (judgment)
