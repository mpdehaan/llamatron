# Music Theory Notes

What Claude knows about how songs are put together, kept by Claude and for Claude, so that the MacroComposer, the song wizard and
the style templates are built on stated principles and not on what is remembered that day. The user may ask for a summary of any
of it written for people.

## The Rules of These Notes (the user's)

- The notes run ahead of the code: a note need not match what is built, and a build is checked against the notes.
- No band, artist, composer, song or other work is named, only genres and subgenres. No melody, riff, bass line or lyric is written down.
- Principles are stated and summarized, high level, in Claude's own words. No source is listed; a reader can search for alternatives.
- What may go in: what public searching finds, general theory, and the theory books the user lent, paraphrased as principles with no
  passage, example or exercise taken. Nothing learned from the homage work goes in.
- Each statement says how sure it is where that matters: **measured** (a study counted it), **practice** (what arrangers and
  players commonly say and do), **judgment** (Claude's own, to be tested by listening).
- Claude adds to a file whenever the work teaches something, and reads the subject's file before building in its area.

## How the Research Is Done (the user's)

- Search one narrow thing at a time, a few words, such as one beat's name or one machine's one feature: a search of many terms
  at once returns thin summaries of everything. Run many small searches side by side.
- Read into the best pages a search finds rather than stopping at the search's summary, and go back with narrower searches for
  whatever a first pass left out.

## The Files

- `SONG_STRUCTURE.md`: sections, forms, lengths, how songs start and end.
- `HOOKS.md`: what a hook is, where hooks go, repetition and variation.
- `MELODY.md`: the ways a melody is made and made interesting.
- `HARMONY.md`: progressions, borrowed chords, chords across sections.
- `RHYTHM_AND_GROOVE.md`: drumming above all: the groove, fills, ghost notes, feel, the kit by section.
- `ARPEGGIOS.md`: arpeggiators and sequenced lines, the arpeggio as a theme, the ostinato score.
- `KEYBOARDS.md`: the organs of rock, what an organist plays, keyboard bass, organ with guitar.
- `DRUM_MACHINES.md`: the machines, what each gave its genres, electronic percussion by style. Machines are named, as products.
- `BAND_INTERPLAY.md`: who answers whom, layering, making room, intensity through a song.
- `TECHNIQUES.md`: things read about and not yet used.
- `genres/`: a file for each genre, with what each subgenre usually does, and for kinds of music not yet a genre of the wizard (`SOUNDTRACK.md`, `JAM.md`, `JAZZ.md`, `COUNTRY.md`, `POP.md`).

## Measuring, and Weighting

- A statement marked (measured) comes from counting chords and sections in the transcriptions of `.corpus/` (chart songs by decade, chords by genre tag, drummers by style), never from melodies,
  and names no song. Measures correct a template's settings: lengths, shares, what a decade or a style really does.
- Averaging many styles together makes mud. A style is a range of habits with shares, and where two habits fight, the style is
  split rather than blended.
- The user weights habits up and down. Weighted up so far: craft in the tune (melody first, a bright tune over a sad story, a hook
  every few bars), and a lead instrument that sings like a voice over a funk groove. Recorded as habits, never as names.
