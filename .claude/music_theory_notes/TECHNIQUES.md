# Techniques Read About and Not Yet Used

Each is a candidate for the MacroComposer, noted so that it is not lost. None is a promise.

- A **pre-chorus** as a kind of section: a ramp of two or four bars with quickening chords and a drum build.
- A **stop**: the whole band silent for a beat or a bar before a chorus, or the tune alone over silence.
- A **key change** up a step or a half step for the last chorus.
- A **dropped or added bar** before a chorus to arrive early or make the listener wait.
- **Half time and double time** sections: the same tempo felt at half or twice the speed, for a bridge or a last chorus.
- A **breakdown**: drums and voice alone, or everything but the drums out, before the last chorus.
- **Pedal point**: the bass holding one note while the chords change above.
- **Harmonized hook**: a second line a third or a sixth from the hook.
- **Trading bars** in a solo, and the band dropping down as a solo begins.
- **Pickups**: a tune or a fill that begins before its section's first bar.
- **Counter-melody**: a slower second tune under the chorus, often on strings, keys or a backing voice.
- **Hi-hat openings, rim clicks and ride bell** as section colors for the drummer.
- **Dynamics inside a phrase**: a swell toward the phrase's high point and away.
- **Layered percussion**: tambourine or shaker entering at the chorus to lift it without changing the drum part.

## Playing From a Lead Sheet

A lead sheet gives a tune, chord symbols and a road map, and nothing else: the band invents the rest by style. What players are
taught to add is what an arranger of generated songs must add. (practice)

- **Read the form first**: repeats, first and second endings, the coda. Then decide an intro (the last four bars, or a vamp on
  the first two chords) and an ending (a tag of the last line three times, a ritard, or a vamp and fade).
- **Bass by style**: pop and country, root on beat 1 and fifth on beat 3, with an eighth-note pickup into each new chord; rock,
  roots in eighths; ballad, roots in half notes; swing, a walking line of chord tones with a chromatic approach into each new
  chord; Latin, root and fifth in the anticipated rhythm. A slash chord is the only bass note the sheet dictates.
- **Chords by style**: pop, block chords or broken chords in the middle register in a repeating rhythm; rock, fifths and octaves
  low, full chords in eighths; jazz, shells (root and seventh, or third and seventh) and rootless voicings with ninths and
  thirteenths, played in short syncopated stabs that answer the tune; ballad, a stride left hand or arpeggios; gospel, full
  chords with walk-ups between them.
- **Leave space and fill it**: comp under the tune's long notes and rests, never on top of its busy bars; a fill in the last bar
  of each phrase, pointing at the next chord.
- **Improve the sheet**: add sevenths where the style wants them; put a ii before a V; approach a chord from a semitone above
  (tritone substitution) or by its own dominant; walk the bass between chords a fourth apart; substitute vi or iii for I to keep a
  long home chord moving; add a passing diminished chord between neighbors a tone apart.
- **Vary by chorus**: first time plain, second time fuller or higher, with a counter-line; a different texture for the bridge;
  hold back for the last verse and open up for the last chorus.
